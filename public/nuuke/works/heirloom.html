01 · The brief

Explain a 900 °C robot plant as rock breathing in.

Heirloom, founded in 2020 by Shashank Samala and Noah McQueen, builds direct air capture plants that use limestone to pull CO₂ out of the air and store it permanently. Its business is selling carbon-removal credits to corporate buyers. The process is a closed calcium loop: heat the rock, capture the CO₂, then let the powder soak up more from the air in about three days.

Design problems we chose

  1. 01

    Make chemistry feel like nature

    A 900 °C, robotic, industrial loop should read as “rock breathing in”, without hiding the industry.

  2. 02

    Prove it’s real

    Sceptical buyers and journalists need permanence, measurement and delivery shown clearly, not just claimed.

  3. 03

    Be a good neighbour

    Louisiana plants are planned. People living nearby need honest answers about jobs, safety and what they’ll see.

Our goal

One scroll that takes anyone from a rock to the whole loop, then sends them to the page made for them.

02 · Audiences & personas

A buyer, a councillor and an engineer.

Heirloom talks to corporate buyers, investors, policymakers, host communities and future hires. We picked three people whose needs pull the site in different directions. Every page has to work for at least one of them.

Personas are Mobile App Creative research assumptions built from the brand’s public audience, not real customers.

Eight beats, four acts, one loop.

One idea per beat

Each beat holds the camera still for most of its scroll distance, so captions can be read. The move happens in the last third, and the caption swaps at the midpoint.

Chemistry as the spine

Three equations carry the story: CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂, then CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂, then Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O. That last step brings you back to rock.

End wide

Beats 1–7 are close-ups. Beat 8 pulls back to the whole diorama, and the conveyor lights up, so the loop is the last thing you see.

05 · Live 3D explainer

Scroll the limestone loop.

This is the experience we’d build for the Technology page, running live. Scroll through eight beats: rock, kiln, split, storage, water, trays, air and loop. Every station is procedural geometry on a single diorama, with no downloaded models or textures.

01/ 08 · Feedstock

Limestone, CaCO₃

It starts with one of the most abundant, cheapest rocks there is. Left alone in nature, limestone absorbs CO₂ from the air over years.

CaCO₃

02/ 08 · Calcination

Heat it to about 900 °C

The rock goes into an electric kiln, powered by renewable electricity, and is heated to about 900 °C.

Kiln0 °C

03/ 08 · The split

Rock in, two things out

Heat splits CaCO₃ into white calcium oxide powder and a pure stream of CO₂. A cement kiln vents that CO₂. Here it is captured.

CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂

04/ 08 · Permanent storage

Locked away for good

The CO₂ goes from the tank to permanent storage. In Tracy it is mineralised into concrete with CarbonCure. The planned Louisiana plants call for geologic storage underground.

Tracy, CA · concrete (CarbonCure)Louisiana · geologic (planned)

05/ 08 · Hydration

Add water

The calcium oxide is hydrated into calcium hydroxide, a white powder that is “thirsty” for CO₂.

CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂

06/ 08 · Spread & stack

Spread thin, stacked tall

The powder is spread thin on hundreds of flat trays, racked about 40 ft (12 m) high and open to the air.

Rack height~0 ft

07/ 08 · Carbonation

Three days of breathing

Air flows through the stacks. The powder soaks up CO₂ like a sponge and turns back into limestone in about 3 days, instead of months or years.

Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O

Day 1Day 2Day 3

08/ 08 · The loop

Back to the kiln, again and again

Robots move along the stacks while software models where each tray is in its cycle. Finished trays return to the kiln, and the same material is reused, cycle after cycle.

Mobile rules

  1. 01

    Tap or swipe, not scrub

    Long pinned scroll fights thumb momentum. On phones each beat is one step, with the scene easing between them.

  2. 02

    Caption as a sheet

    The 3D takes the top 55% of the screen. The caption sits below it, so text never covers the subject.

  3. 03

    Half the particles

    Particle counts halve under 760 px, with the same shaders and draw calls. The scene pauses when it is off-screen.

  4. 04

    Thumb-zone controls

    Back and next sit at the bottom in 48 px targets. The progress bar at the top is also tappable.

This is the live scene running in the phone, so tap through it. It advances by itself until you touch it.

Load only when needed

Three.js is a separate chunk, imported only when the explainer gets within 900 px of the viewport. The renderer caps pixel ratio at 1.75 and stops rendering when the canvas is off-screen or the tab is hidden. Phones get half the particles.

Reduced motion

With prefers-reduced-motion, nothing is pinned or scrubbed. The explainer becomes a fixed panel with eight step buttons, each showing a still frame. A GPU-less device gets the same static frames. If WebGL fails, the storyboard drawings stand in.

Captions carry the story

Every beat is real HTML text: a heading, a paragraph and the formula. The canvas is decorative, so screen readers get the full story in order. The beat steps are buttons with labels and the current one is marked. Live readouts (temperature, height, days) duplicate what the 3D shows.

Honest motion

Scroll position alone sets the camera and every station. Time only drives ambient flicker and particles. Scrolling back rewinds the loop exactly, and a skipped frame never desynchronises the story.

10 · Measure

How we’d know it worked.

KPI 01

Loop completion

We expect: Most visitors who start the explainer reach beat 8 and the full-loop view.

How we’d measure: Beat-reached events (1–8) from the scroll trigger, split by device.

KPI 02

Three-minute understanding

We expect: After one pass, test users can explain kiln → storage → trays → kiln in their own words.

How we’d measure: Five-question comprehension test with recruits like Engineer Lin, run before and after.

KPI 03

Buyer readiness

We expect: Buyers who visit the permanence and measurement sections ask fewer basic questions on sales calls.

How we’d measure: Buyers-page path analysis plus a short tag in the CRM on first calls.

KPI 04

Neighbour trust

We expect: The road-view and FAQ cut repeat questions at community sessions.

How we’d measure: FAQ “was this helpful” votes, question themes logged at sessions, and session sign-ups.

KPI 05

Smoothness

We expect: The explainer holds 60 fps on recent laptops and stays smooth on mid-range phones.

How we’d measure: Real-user frame timing and Core Web Vitals (LCP, INP) by device class.

These are the success measures we would agree with the client before launch.

01 · The brief

Giving food away should be faster than binning it.

Olio began with a failed handover: on moving day in 2014, co-founder Tessa Clarke couldn’t find anyone to take her good food. With Saasha Celestial-One she founded Olio in London in 2015, an app where neighbours give away, request, borrow and lend food and household things, mostly for free. Businesses pay Olio so that trained volunteer Food Waste Heroes collect their unsold food and list it in the app. Its mission: a world where “nothing of value goes to waste”. Its 2023 rebrand traded green for warm colours and an idea it calls “urgent optimism”.

Design problems we chose

  1. 01

    Speed is the product

    Good food goes in minutes. Discovery and alerts must surface local, time-critical items fairly, without notification overload.

  2. 02

    Trust between strangers

    The doorstep handover has to feel safe and polite on both sides: clear pickup etiquette, visible reliability, no awkward no-shows.

  3. 03

    Make the volunteer engine scale

    A Food Waste Hero bag holds 10–40 items. Listing it should be a 60-second job, not thirty forms.

Our goal

Photo to live in under a minute, reserved in minutes, collected without awkwardness, for one loaf or a whole bag.

02 · Research

Three neighbours, one shared clock.

Olio is a two-sided market that happens on a doorstep. We modelled a collector, a giver and a volunteer from Olio’s public audience, then timed every step of their journeys. For all three, the enemy is the same: minutes.

Personas are Mobile App Creative research assumptions built from the brand’s public audience, not real customers.

Both lanes · the middle of the journey

The doorstep handover

What happens

Sam knocks (or collects from the porch). Diane hands over the bag. Twenty seconds between two strangers.

Insight

This is the moment of truth for trust. If it feels awkward or unsafe, neither comes back.

Opportunity

Address revealed only after reserving, clear etiquette (“contactless porch pickup OK”), and a pickup window both agreed in chat.

06 · 3D wireframe

The Nearby feed, taken apart.

Five layers make the home feed. Scroll to assemble it, and drag to look around. Notice how little is purple: we kept it for the actions that make sharing happen.

Scroll to assemble · drag to orbit

  1. L1

    Layout grid

    4-column grid, 20pt margins and an 8pt rhythm. The thumb zone (bottom third) is reserved for Add and Request.

  2. L2

    Wireframe

    Freshest first: one large “just added” card, then compact rows. Distance and age sit on every card.

  3. L3

    Content

    Real copy with a clock: “2 min ago · 350 m”, a pickup window, and a single verb on the button.

  4. L4

    Colour & type

    Warm on oat: coral marks freshness, purple is reserved for the one action that matters (Add / Request).

  5. L5

    Motion & states

    New listings pulse once, the age ticks up live, and a card flips to “Reserved” the moment a neighbour is chosen.

09 · Measure

How we’d know it got faster.

KPI 01

Time to list

We expect: AI-ify plus remembered pickup times bring a food listing from photo to live in under a minute.

How we’d measure: Median and p90 time from opening the camera to publish, split by first vs. repeat listings.

KPI 02

Time to reserve, without the noise

We expect: Fair waves get food reserved within the first wave or two while sending each neighbour fewer alerts.

How we’d measure: Publish → accepted request time by density; alerts per user and opt-out rate. A/B: waves vs. broadcast.

KPI 03

Pickup completion

We expect: Agreed windows, a clear “Reserved” state and reminders reduce no-shows.

How we’d measure: Share of reserved listings marked collected; no-show reports per 100 pickups.

KPI 04

Volunteer batch time

We expect: A Food Waste Hero can list a whole bag in a couple of minutes.

How we’d measure: Time from “Add Collection” to all items live; items unclaimed at the end of the window.

These are the success measures we would agree with the client before launch.

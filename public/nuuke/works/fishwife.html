01 · The brief

Tinned fish has a fan club. We want it to have a lunch hour.

Fishwife is a Los Angeles tinned-seafood brand, founded by Becca Millstein and Caroline Goldfarb and launched in December 2020. It sells premium, responsibly sourced fish in illustrated tins, packed by six canneries in Washington State, Spain, Denmark and Scotland. Its stated mission is to make tinned seafood “a staple in every cupboard.”

Design problems we chose

  1. 01

    From special to staple

    The tin is loved as a snack-plate, hosting and gifting item. Those moments happen a few times a month. Lunch happens five times a week.

  2. 02

    Give the shelf a reason

    With national grocery distribution, every tin now competes in the lunch basket. Shoppers need a cue to pick one up on a Tuesday.

  3. 03

    Scale the world, keep the hand

    More creators, more posts, more stores, without diluting the hand-drawn, maximalist world that made people notice the brand.

Our goal

Make 12:30 on a weekday the moment people reach for a tin.

02 · Insight & audience

People already love the tin. They just save it for company.

Our insight comes from how the category is talked about (snack plates, “girl dinner”, gifting), not from client data. It’s a hypothesis we’d validate with social listening and a short survey before launch.

Tension

Tinned fish is having a cultural moment, but mostly as a special-occasion food: boards, parties, gifts.

Truth

The things people love about it (no cooking, no fridge, real protein, a pretty object) are exactly what a weekday desk lunch lacks.

Opportunity

Don’t sell a new product. Give an existing love a new time slot, and make that time impossible to forget.

Who we’re talking to

Personas are Mobile App Creative research assumptions built from the brand’s public audience, not real customers.

07 · Creator kit

A brief creators actually want to read.

Creators bring real desks and real hands. The kit keeps every post on-ritual: one card, a bank of hooks, and clear rules.

12:30

Creator brief · Wave 1

Show us your Tin O’Clock.

One weekday lunch, one tin, under 30 seconds. Make it look like the best part of your workday, because it is.

Deliverables

3 × story frames

#TinOClock · the 12:30 alarm sound

Approvals
Script outline → rough cut → final.
Claims checked against the fact sheet.

Need a hook?

“It’s 12:30 and I have exactly 30 seconds.”

Do

  • Film at a real desk, in real light, at lunchtime.
  • Open the tin on camera; the ring-pull sound is the hook.
  • Keep it under 30 seconds and say the flavour out loud.
  • Show the tin as the plate. No extra dishes needed.
  • Label every paid post clearly (#ad / paid partnership).

Don’t

  • Make health, nutrition or sustainability claims beyond the approved fact sheet.
  • Stage a kitchen you don’t have. The desk is the point.
  • Hide the tin behind props or other brands.
  • Script fake reviews, “results” or before/after body claims.
  • Post the Tin O’Clock content outside the lunch window.

The kit protects the illustrated world: creators bring the real desk and real hands; the brand brings the colour, lettering and the tin.

11 · Budget & measurement

Where the money goes, and how we’d know.

Pick a preset or drag the sliders to see how the mix shifts. Below are the measures we’d agree before launch. They are expectations, not results.

Big first fortnight: OOH and creators create the moment.

Biggest slice26%Creators & UGC

Shares of working media, normalised to 100%. Illustrative: the total budget is set with the client.

What we’d measure

KPI 01

Weekday-lunch purchase share

We expect: A rising share of orders and scans lands Monday to Friday, 11:00–14:00, against a pre-campaign baseline.

How we’d measure: DTC order timestamps plus retailer and receipt-panel data by daypart.

KPI 02

Saves & shares rate

We expect: Recipe shorts earn more saves and shares per 1,000 views than the account’s own baseline.

How we’d measure: Platform analytics by format; saves are our proxy for “I’ll make this for lunch”.

KPI 03

Retail velocity

We expect: Units per store per week grow faster in shelf-takeover stores than in matched stores without it.

How we’d measure: Retailer point-of-sale data, test vs. control stores over the flight.

KPI 04

Ritual adoption

We expect: 12:30 alarm opt-ins repeat-purchase more often within 30 days than non-opted buyers.

How we’d measure: SMS opt-in cohort vs. matched non-opted cohort, plus #TinOClock duet volume.

These are the success measures we would agree with the client before launch.

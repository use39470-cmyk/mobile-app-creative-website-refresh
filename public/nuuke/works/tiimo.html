01 · The brief

A planner for brains that work differently.

Tiimo is a visual planner from Copenhagen, founded in 2015 by Helene Lassen Nørlem and Melissa Würtz Azari. It began as a research project interviewing neurodivergent teenagers about how technology could support them at school. Today it turns to-dos into colour-coded timeline blocks, with an AI co-planner, a visual focus timer and mood check-ins. Its own line: “Designed for brains that work differently.”

Design problems we chose

  1. 01

    Make time visible, without panic

    For people with time blindness, the passing of time has to be seen. But a ticking countdown can feel like an alarm. The shape of time must calm, not chase.

  2. 02

    AI that doesn’t overwhelm

    A co-planner that turns one task into twelve has made things worse. Its output needs pacing, sensible defaults and an undo that’s always in reach.

  3. 03

    Sensory settings as a system

    Theme colours, fonts and motion are personal. Whatever someone picks, contrast, layout and reduced motion must still hold.

Our goal

A day you can see is a day you can start. Make the plan visible in one glance, and gentle enough to come back to tomorrow.

03 · Journey map

Jess’s Tuesday, and where the app can help.

Tap each moment to see what Jess is doing, how she feels, and the design opportunity we found. The low points shaped the whole product.

Stage 1 · Wake

Doing

Wakes with a dozen to-dos already in her head.

Feeling

😕 Overwhelmed before she has started.

Opportunity

One brain-dump box on the lock screen: get it out of her head first, sort it later.

Stage 2 · Plan

Doing

Tries to order the list; everything feels urgent.

Feeling

😣 Stuck, and starting to avoid it.

Opportunity

The co-planner proposes an order and soft time estimates, all editable, with undo.

Stage 3 · Start

Doing

Opens the deck file, closes it, opens email.

Feeling

😕 Can’t get going.

Opportunity

Show only the first tiny step and a focus disc. Starting should take one tap.

Stage 4 · Focus

Doing

Gets into flow and loses track of time.

Feeling

🙂 Good, until lunch is late.

Opportunity

A haptic tick every 5 minutes and a Live Activity disc on the lock screen.

Stage 5 · Running late

Doing

Lunch overruns by 40 minutes.

Feeling

😕 Ashamed; tempted to give up on the plan.

Opportunity

The timeline shifts gently. No red, no “overdue”, just “here’s your afternoon now”.

Stage 6 · Evening

Doing

Looks back at the day.

Feeling

😄 Calm; it was a day she could see.

Opportunity

An optional mood check-in that celebrates what got done, never a broken streak.

05 · Wireframes

Watch the screens draw themselves.

Every screen starts as pencil, becomes grey boxes, then gets colour and content. Scroll to replay that process on three key screens; the design notes pin on as they were made.

  1. Sketch
  2. Wireframe
  3. Colour
  4. Notes
  1. 1One directionThe day reads top to bottom, one column. No grid of hours to decode.
  2. 2Emoji firstYou recognise a task by its picture before you read it, which helps dyslexic readers.
  3. 3A moving “now”The now line glides down. Nothing flashes or turns red when you run late.
  4. 4Dump from anywhereThe + opens brain dump on every screen, so a thought never has to wait.
  1. 1No form to fillBrain dump is a single box. Type, paste or speak; structure comes later.
  2. 2AI suggests, you decideThe co-planner proposes steps. Each one is editable, and nothing is added until you say so.
  3. 3Estimates, not deadlinesTimes are soft chips. Tap one to change it; nothing turns into a countdown yet.
  4. 4Undo, alwaysIf the breakdown feels like too much, one tap puts the original task back.
  1. 1Time you can seeThe disc drains as time passes. For time blindness, the shape matters more than the numbers.
  2. 2Tiny next stepOnly the current sub-step is bold. The rest wait quietly below.
  3. 3Extend without guilt“+5 min” is the same size as “Done”. Running over is normal, not a failure.
  4. 4Off the phone tooThe same disc becomes a Live Activity, so the timer keeps going on the lock screen.

06 · 3D layers

The Today screen, taken apart.

Five layers make one calm screen. Scroll and the exploded view assembles into the final UI; drag to look around it.

Scroll to assemble · drag to orbit

  1. L1

    Layout & safe areas

    One 64pt time gutter, one content column, 12pt rhythm. Safe areas for the island, the + button and the tab bar.

  2. L2

    Wireframe

    Grey boxes first: date strip, then blocks whose height follows their duration, then a single + button.

  3. L3

    Content

    Real tasks, real times. Labels stay under 20 characters so they never wrap to a second line.

  4. L4

    Colour & type

    Each task keeps its own pastel. Ink text on every pastel stays above 7:1, whatever intensity you pick.

  5. L5

    Now line & motion

    The now line glides 1pt a minute. The current block gets a soft glow and a draining disc. Reduce Motion turns both into still states.

          11 · Measure

          How we’d know it’s working.

          KPI 01

          Time to first plan

          We expect: New users place a first day on the timeline within 3 minutes of install.

          How we’d measure: Onboarding funnel timing, install → first timeline with 3+ blocks.

          KPI 02

          Co-planner trust

          We expect: Most suggested steps are kept or lightly edited; undo is used without people leaving.

          How we’d measure: Accept / edit / undo rate per suggestion, and next-day return after an undo.

          KPI 03

          Timers finished

          We expect: Gentler late states and +5 min reduce abandoned focus sessions.

          How we’d measure: Focus completion rate before and after v1.1, A/B on late-state copy.

          KPI 04

          Settings that stick

          We expect: People who tune a sensory setting in week 1 keep planning in week 4.

          How we’d measure: Week-4 retention by settings cohort; survey on comfort.

          These are the success measures we would agree with the client before launch.

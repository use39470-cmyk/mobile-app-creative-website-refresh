01 · The brief · Discover

Make bad news feel boringly obvious.

Flighty is a flight tracker for iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch and Apple Vision, founded by Ryan Jones, a former Apple operations employee. A six-hour delay pushed him to tweet that he’d build something better himself; his co-founders came from the replies. It launched in 2019 and combines live flight data with FAA and air-traffic-control data. The promise is simple: delay alerts that arrive faster than the airline’s own. The team’s own bar: “work so well that it feels almost boringly obvious.”

Design problems we chose

  1. 01

    Calm under bad news

    A cancellation or diversion has to feel urgent but never panicky, and lead with the next action: rebook.

  2. 02

    One hierarchy, every surface

    Watch, Live Activity, widget and Mac each need the same reading order: one line per flight, like a departure board.

  3. 03

    Shareable by design

    Flighty grows without paid marketing. Passport and Friends could become richer, privacy-safe moments worth sharing.

Our goal

Every surface answers two questions in one glance: is my flight OK, and what do I do next?

02 · Research

Three travellers, one departure board.

We built personas from Flighty’s public audience: frequent flyers, business travellers, aviation fans and the families who track them. Then we studied the interface every traveller already knows how to read.

Personas are Mobile App Creative research assumptions built from the brand’s public audience, not real customers.

05 · 3D wireframe

Take the flight card apart.

Five layers, from grid to motion. Scroll and the exploded view assembles into the final card. Drag to look around it.

Scroll to assemble · drag to orbit

  1. L1

    Layout grid

    A 4-column grid with 20pt margins, an 8pt rhythm, and safe areas for the Dynamic Island and the home indicator.

  2. L2

    Wireframe

    The departure-board hierarchy in grey boxes: route, then times, then one status line, then gate. Everything else waits below the fold.

  3. L3

    Content & data

    Real data in real lengths. Codes are biggest, times second. The status line answers “why?” in plain words, and nothing reads like a percentage.

  4. L4

    Colour & status

    Near-black like a board at night. Colour is only ever status: green on time, amber delayed, red cancelled, always paired with a word.

  5. L5

    Motion & states

    Changed values flip like split-flap tiles, so you notice what moved. Old times and gates stay visible, struck through, for one glance.

07 · Making of · Lock screen & island

Design for the screen nobody opens.

Most of a travel day happens on the lock screen and in the Dynamic Island. We designed those surfaces on a bench: switch the flight state and watch the Live Activity and all three island sizes update together, then try the one-second glance test.

Dynamic Island

Signage rule for this state

Gate first, time second.

At the airport, the two things a board prints biggest are where to go and when. The compact island keeps only those two.

Apple describes Flighty’s Live Activities and Dynamic Island as designed to recall airport signage conventions that have been in place for decades.Apple, Behind the Design

Our bench rules

  • One line per flight. If it doesn’t fit a board row, it doesn’t go in the island.
  • Fixed positions. The gate is always bottom right; times never swap sides.
  • Colour plus a word. Amber always says “Delayed”, for colour-blind eyes and bright sun.
  • Tabular numbers. Times update in place without jiggling.

Illustrative numbers. They show how we’d run and read a 5-person moderated test. The session is scripted for this concept, not recorded from real users.

  1. Finding 01

    Testers tapped the amber status chip, expecting the reason. It did nothing, so they went hunting through tabs.

    Design changeThe status line became the button: “Delayed 48 min · Why? ›” opens the explanation in one tap.

  2. Finding 02

    The gate had changed, but the new gate looked identical to the old one. Two testers walked (on paper) to B12.

    Design changeGate changes now take the top slot, old gate struck through, new gate in signage amber, with walk time.

  3. Finding 03

    Nobody connected “late inbound aircraft” to “Where’s my plane”. It lived three levels deep behind a ••• menu.

    Design changeThe inbound plane now appears inside the delay card, with its position and a link to its 25-hour day.

11 · Ship & measure

How we’d know it worked.

KPI 01

Head start

We expect: Users hear about delays meaningfully earlier than the airline’s own update, on most delayed flights.

How we’d measure: First alert timestamp vs. the airline’s first status change, per delayed flight.

KPI 02

One-second read

We expect: At least 9 in 10 testers read gate and time from the Live Activity in a one-second glance.

How we’d measure: The glance test from chapter 07, run per state in moderated sessions.

KPI 03

Calm cancellations

We expect: After a cancellation, people reach rebooking options faster and contact support less.

How we’d measure: Time from cancel alert to rebook tap; rebook completion; a short stress rating.

KPI 04

Shared, not bought

We expect: Passport posters and Friends invites bring new installs without paid media.

How we’d measure: Share-sheet completions and installs attributed to share links.

These are the success measures we would agree with the client before launch.

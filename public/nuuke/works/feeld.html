01 · Discover · The brief

A dating app where every yes has to be mutual.

Feeld is a London dating app “for the curious”: singles, couples and constellations of partners who are “open to experiencing people and relationships in new ways.” Designer Dimo Trifonov launched it in July 2014 as 3nder, after his then-partner Ana Kirova told him she had feelings for other people; a trademark suit from Tinder’s parent company led to the name Feeld in 2016. Kirova joined in 2016 and has been CEO since 2021. Members choose from 20+ genders and 20+ sexualities, keep a public bio and a hidden one, and can link their profile with up to five people. It’s ad-free and promises it “will never rank profiles.” In 2023 a rebrand by Made Thought, with type by Charlotte Rohde, gave it an Aura of heat-map gradients and motion modelled on breath, heartbeat and touch.

Design problems we chose

  1. 01

    Identity without boxes

    Twenty-plus genders, twenty-plus sexualities and fifty-plus desires have to feel like a conversation, not a form. Nine in ten members update their bios, so change has to be easy too.

  2. 02

    Consent you can see

    Hidden bios, private photos and chat open at different moments. People should always know what a like shares, and that the closest layers open only on a mutual yes.

  3. 03

    Privacy you can feel

    In Feeld’s own survey, 58% of member respondents said they highly value control over their personal information. Incognito, blur and private photos should show their effect, not hide in settings.

Our goal

Every layer of a profile says who can see it, and the closest layers open only when both people say yes.

08 · Design · 3D wireframe

The profile screen, taken apart.

Each screen is built in five layers, from grid to consent states and motion. Scroll and the exploded view assembles into the final UI; drag to look around it.

Scroll to assemble · drag to orbit

  1. L1

    Layout grid

    4-column grid, 20pt margins and an 8pt rhythm. The profile is three stacked layers: public, chosen, mutual.

  2. L2

    Wireframe

    Hierarchy first: one portrait, identity in their words, then the layers that open with consent, each labelled with who can see it.

  3. L3

    Content & language

    Real copy in the member’s own words. Identity chips are plural, never a binary. Every hidden layer says why it is hidden.

  4. L4

    Colour & aura

    Ink and bone carry the UI. The aura gradient is reserved for emotive moments: the portrait, a like, a mutual connection.

  5. L5

    Consent & motion

    Locks lift only on a mutual yes. The reveal breathes in over 5.5 s, the like button beats once, and nothing ever moves on its own twice.

Components

The aura rule

The gradient appears only for emotive moments (a like, a mutual connection), as Feeld’s identity intends. Everything else is ink and bone.

Consent layer

Hidden bioOpens if Sam likes you

Every hidden layer names who can see it and why. Locked layers use a dashed edge; open ones turn solid.

Identity chips

✓ QueerBisexual✓ Curious

Multi-select by default. Never a binary toggle, never a required field.

Motion

Breath (5.5 s) for calm states, one heartbeat for a like, a slow merge for mutual. All of it stops with Reduce Motion.

Hex values are our working approximation of the 2023 Aura identity as described publicly (heat-map gradients in pink, purple, orange and green on black and white); Feeld publishes no official values. Feeld’s real typefaces are a custom New Edge 666 Rounded by Charlotte Rohde and ABC Diatype. We use Montserrat and Inter as stand-ins and don’t recreate the wordmark.

12 · Ship · Measure

How we’d know it worked.

KPI 01

Consent is legible

We expect: After one use, people can say what a like shares and what opens on a mutual yes, without help.

How we’d measure: Comprehension questions after the mutual-reveal task, five people per round, until 5 of 5 answer correctly.

KPI 02

Identity without drop-off

We expect: More people finish the identity step, and more of them pick several words or add their own.

How we’d measure: Funnel from identity start to finish; share of multi-select and custom entries, by cohort.

KPI 03

Privacy controls found and trusted

We expect: People find Incognito in under ten seconds and correctly predict who can see them.

How we’d measure: First-click test, plus a prediction task scored against the visibility matrix.

KPI 04

Feeling safe, for everyone

We expect: Self-reported safety rises, with no gap for trans and non-binary members.

How we’d measure: Short in-app pulse survey each quarter, segmented by gender identity, with an opt-out.

KPI 05

Linked profiles that hold

We expect: Most link invites are accepted within the 72-hour window, and few are unlinked within a week.

How we’d measure: Invite acceptance and early-unlink rates; follow-up interviews with couples and constellations.

These are the success measures we would agree with the client before launch.

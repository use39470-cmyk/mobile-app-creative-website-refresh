01 · The brief

A kettle that's famous for its silhouette.

Fellow is a San Francisco design brand founded by Jake Miller in 2013. Its tagline is “Everyday Magic.” The Stagg EKG is its electric pour-over kettle: a squat cylinder with a flat lid, a long gooseneck and a counterbalanced handle, set on a square base with a small display and one dial. It holds 0.9 L, draws 1200 W, sets any temperature from 135–212 °F and holds it for up to 60 minutes.

Design problems we chose

  1. 01

    Make precision visible

    Temperature, flow and balance are the EKG’s real benefits, and all three are invisible on camera. The film needs a visual language for each.

  2. 02

    One hero object, many finishes

    Black, white, copper, stone blue with walnut. A modular CGI pipeline keeps every colourway and future launch consistent and fast.

  3. 03

    Calm, not loud

    The audience buys slow rituals. The edit has to move at the pace of a pour, not a sneaker ad.

Our goal

A 60-second launch film that makes precision visible, built on one 3D scene that renders every colourway and every cutdown.

02 · Audience

Three people who'd watch a kettle film to the end.

Fellow’s public audience is specialty home brewers, design-led gift buyers and tea drinkers. Each persona gets at least one shot written for them.

Personas are Mobile App Creative research assumptions built from the brand’s public audience, not real customers.

03 · Creative concept

Everyday Magic, made of light.

Before modelling anything we agree a mood: dark seamless studio, one warm rim, slow motion. The moodboard is built entirely in code, from palette, material and type cards, so nothing is borrowed.

The line we build on

Motion

The pour sets the pace.

One review measured a ~1–2 g/s trickle. Slow in, slow out; no whip pans.

Light = heat. The rim warms and the element glows as the reading climbs.

Ring = time. Hold mode draws a 60-minute circle around the base.

Dot = balance. A centre-of-mass marker shows why it doesn’t nose-dive.

05 · Modelling & look dev

From grey boxes to the poster frame.

The kettle on this page is modelled in code: a lathed body, a swept, tapering gooseneck and a tube-swept handle. Step through the pipeline, switch colourways, and drag to orbit.

What we model to (published specs)

  • 0.9 L capacity
  • 1200 W (US)
  • 135–212 °F / 57–100 °C, to the degree
  • 60 min Hold mode
  • 304 stainless steel body and lid

Temperature range is Fellow’s current spec. Some older sources list a lower minimum; it varies by model and version, so the film only shows 135–212 °F.

Loading the studio…

01 · Blockout. Grey primitives only. We lock proportions and camera distances against the published 292 × 171 × 203 mm footprint before any detail exists.

02 · Wireframe. Clean, light topology: a lathed body, a swept gooseneck that tapers to a pointed tip, and a tube-swept counterbalanced handle. Edge loops go where light will catch.

03 · Clay. One neutral material. Clay shows form, silhouette and shadow problems that colour would hide, so the client signs off shape here.

04 · Materials. Flat, even light so we judge shaders, not lighting: matte powder coat, polished copper, walnut. One shader set, switched per colourway.

05 · Lighting. Key, fill and two rims (softboxes shown). The rim is the hero: it draws the gooseneck line against black.

06 · Final. The poster frame: a lifted pour into a cone, a thin stream, steam and a warm backdrop glow. Same model, same shaders.

Clay vs final

Same model, same camera, same lights.

Both halves are rendered live from the scene above, one with the clay shader and one with the final look. Drag the handle: only the surfaces change. That’s the point of a modular pipeline: a new colourway is a shader swap, not a reshoot.

10 · Measure

How we'd know the film worked.

KPI 01

View-through rate

We expect: A slow, beat-led edit holds people to the pour (shot 8), not just the reveal.

How we’d measure: Completion at 25/50/75/100 % on the 60 s and 15 s cuts, by placement.

KPI 02

Colourway clicks

We expect: The carousel and end card send viewers to a specific colourway, not the generic product page.

How we’d measure: Click-through from end card and video description to each colourway page, tagged per cut.

KPI 03

Precision recall

We expect: Viewers remember “to the degree” and “60-minute hold”, not just “nice kettle”.

How we’d measure: Brand-lift survey against a hold-out group, aided and unaided recall of the three features.

KPI 04

Asset reuse

We expect: One scene file covers every colourway and format without reshoots.

How we’d measure: Count of deliverables rendered from the master scene versus new production days.

These are the success measures we would agree with the client before launch.
